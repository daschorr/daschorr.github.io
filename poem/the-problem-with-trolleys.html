D. A. Schorr

poet | he / him

The Problem With Trolleys

When the glass broke, it was because my student placed his fist first on one side and then the other, shattering the barrier placed between him and the accomplishments of decades of students who came before. When the glass broke, it was because he learned his girlfriend had been pressing something sharp to the inside of her thigh again and it was because at sixteen neither of them knew how to hold her sadness flat enough on their own. After the glass broke, he sat for a time on the floor of the art room, cradling his hand, and it was because the red dripping between his fingers and across his knuckles was a bright crimson that I thought, at first, he had been painting. After the glass broke, because his mom was a few blocks away, she begged me not to call an ambulance. Because a school has a duty of care, it was on its way and out of my hands. And maybe it was kinder because neither of us had to decide. Two weeks before the glass broke, my student raised his unholey hand and said, The problem with trolleys is that -- OK I'm the driver, I've got two choices: left or right. But who built the track? Who scheduled the workers? Who left me alone with my hands on a lever?

This poem was awarded an honorable mention in the 2026 New Milford Poetry Contest.
