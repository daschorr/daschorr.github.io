D. A. Schorr

poet | he / him

My Dog Speaks;
Or, Telemachus To Odysseus

I have waited twenty years to hear your tires on the gravel and the scrape of your key, to smell you, to place what hands I have on your knee. I know you depended on me each of these twenty long years to defend our home from all those who would take it. I have tried my best to hold my own against every foe. I have barked at the little ones that come from the trees, at the larger ones that run smugly on two legs, at one hundred and eight leaves drifting shiftily, conspicuously across the places where you have not been for twenty years. But now I hear your tires on the gravel. Now I lift myself from where I was curled by the door. Now I place what hands I have on your knee and howl. Now you hold your forehead to mine, war-worn and weary, like you buried your guilt and will dig up the whole yard to find it. What happened to you that delayed you all these years? What beasts did you battle as you sailed far from our lawn? Whatever impossible choices you made, you made it back home. My prodigal father, you have returned -- what a joy it is to be alive. Would you peel a trunk of broccoli for me, the way that you used to, so that I might take it beneath the bed and cry?

This poem was awarded first place in the 2026 New Milford Poetry Contest.
